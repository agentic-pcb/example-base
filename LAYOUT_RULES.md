# Layout rules

Generic schematic and PCB layout rules for any tscircuit board (`*.circuit.tsx`): where parts, copper, labels and schematic symbols go. Which parts and values to use and how to wire them is in the companion file `CIRCUIT_RULES.md`. A rule belongs there when it can be checked from the netlist and the BOM alone, and here when it needs coordinates, copper, layers, silkscreen or the schematic drawing. This file is the single source of truth, referenced by URL; it is not copied into projects. Rules: alignment (1-13), routing (14-17 and 39-40), mounting (18), schematic (19-20 and 29-36), placement (21-27, 37-38 and 41-42), silkscreen groups (43), outline (28), part-specific layout (44-60), manufacturing (61-63) and layers and vias (64-66). Rules 1-43 are project conventions; a rule or clause with a source tag `[S#]` comes from the Sources list of `CIRCUIT_RULES.md`. A `Don't:` clause at the end of a rule is a counter-example: it names the mistake that breaks the rule and adds no rule number; check a layout against it as well. Schematic rules change layout only (positions, rotation, pin side and order, sheet size, trace labels), never the netlist or the parts, so they can always be met in the `*.circuit.tsx`. A board whose geometry is fixed by its parts (e.g. an LED matrix) may be exempt; say so in the project README, together with every other exception. Where two rules pull against each other: the datasheet of the actual part beats this file, a part-specific rule (44-60) beats a generic one (1-43), the manufacturing rules (61-63) are limits of the assembly process and hold beside all others, and among the generic rules the electrical one wins (decoupling, rule 24, over via avoidance, rule 15, and over equal spacing, rules 10 and 11); on the schematic a correct, readable connection wins over compactness.

**Every board is a 2-layer board:** copper on the top and the bottom side only (`<board layers={2}>`), joined by plated through vias. Not single-sided: a 1-layer board has no plated holes at JLCPCB, so no vias and no ground pour under the parts [S56]. Not multilayer: no board of 4 to 32 layers, no inner plane, no blind or buried via. Every rule below is written for two layers and names the top and the bottom layer directly. Rules 64-66 say how the two layers are used and what vias are for: on two layers a via is the tool that lets a trace cross another, brings every ground pad to the pour and carries heat to the other side, at no extra cost. No rule is ever met by adding a layer; a design that does not fit on two layers is a question for the user (rule 64).

## Alignment rules

1. **Share baselines:** Put pin rows of neighboring modules, headers, and connectors on one common top, bottom, or center line; do the same for small parts (a row of resistors or caps shares one center line).
2. **Keep margins equal:** Give the left and right edges the same margin, and the top and bottom edges the same margin.
3. **Place on a grid:** Put every part on one grid, such as 1.27 mm or 2.54 mm.
4. **Use one orientation per part type:** Face all resistors, caps, and ICs the same way, and rotate parts only by 0° and 90°.
5. **Keep pitch constant:** Space equal parts and pads evenly, and use one pitch per connector row.
6. **Use one pad size per function:** Give all connector pads one diameter and one drill, and all header pads one diameter and one drill.
7. **Print labels on one baseline:** Use one text size and one baseline for pad labels, and one smaller size for designators, on the same side of every part. Text reads horizontally; where space forces a turn, turn all such text the same way (bottom to top), never mix. Module names are horizontal too unless the module is narrower than its name.
8. **Place labels next to their parts:** Put each label directly beside its part, clear of pads, outlines, and vias. Put designators above their parts; one moves only when the courtyard forbids it, and then every designator of that cluster moves.
9. **Label every pad:** Give each pad and connector a short, unambiguous pin label, and each pad group a group label (rule 43).
10. **Space blocks equally:** Keep the same gap between modules and between groups of parts.
11. **Distribute space evenly:** Spread the parts across the board, or shrink the board until the free space is balanced.
12. **Inset mounting holes equally:** Place all four the same distance from the corners, with clearance from pads.
13. **Align edge parts to one line:** Place parts near an edge at one shared distance from it, and put edge connectors flush with the edge on purpose.

## Routing rules

14. **Double width for power lines:** Draw every power line (any voltage net such as 5V, 3V3, VCC, VDD, VIN, VBUS) and GND at least twice as wide as the standard (signal) trace. The trace at a fuse must carry the fused current.
15. **Avoid vias on power lines:** Try not to use vias on any power line (5V, 3V3, VCC, VDD, VIN, VBUS, every supply net other than GND); route them on one layer, the top one, where possible; Espressif asks the same of a two-layer board: power on the top layer, a via to the bottom only when it cannot be avoided [S20]. A via on a power line at a decoupling capacitor or a regulator is accepted when the alternative is a longer path (rule 24 wins); a power line that has to change layer does so through at least two vias (rule 66). This rule covers supply nets only: signals and GND use vias freely (rule 65). Don't: read this rule as "avoid vias" and detour a signal or leave a GND pad on a long trace to save one. (convention)
16. **Pour GND copper:** Try to fill the free area of the bottom layer of the board with a GND copper pour; do not pour on the top layer. This pour is the only ground plane the board has (rule 64: no inner layer), so keep it in one piece. Connect every top-layer GND pad to it with a GND via beside the pad (rules 61 and 65). Keep the pour clear of the board edge (rule 26) and of any antenna or keep-out area. Don't: split the ground into separate analog and digital planes (rule 57 has the one exception), or route a trace across a slot or gap in the pour: the return current flows directly under its trace, and in EMC tests most problems come from traces that cross a split [S46]. Don't let long bottom-layer traces cut the pour into strips: cross on the bottom with a short hop between two vias (rule 65) (convention).
17. **Avoid 90 degree corners:** Try not to turn a trace by 90 degrees; make every corner two 45 degree bends instead. A T junction (a trace joining another one) is allowed. A short cut (down to 0.3 mm) where a pad is close, or a bend inside a pad, is accepted. For hand-routed copper (`<trace pcbPath>`) write a small helper that cuts each corner; check autorouted copper in the render and fix any 90 degree corner with an explicit `pcbPath`. Don't: join traces at an acute angle (under 90 degrees) or leave a small, nearly closed pocket between copper shapes: etchant can stay trapped there (acid trap) and corrode the copper into an open circuit; the trapped pocket is the cause, a sharp corner is only its usual sign (secondary). [S50]

## Mounting rules

18. **Keep the screw head area free:** Around every mounting hole keep a free circle of twice the screw head width: 3.5 mm hole, 3 mm screw (5.5 mm head), 6 mm free diameter, concentric with the hole. Place no component, pad or silkscreen text inside it, as far as the board allows. Traces and the copper pour may run through it.

## Schematic rules

19. **Group by function:** Give every functional block (e.g. MCU, power, outputs, input) its own `<schematicsection>` (`schSectionName`) on the one sheet and keep its parts clustered by `schX`/`schY`, with a clear gap between blocks. The installed `tsci` draws no section title or frame (`<schematictext>`, `<schematicrect>` and `<schematicbox>` do not render), so the clustering and the gap are the only visible grouping. Add a second sheet only when a block no longer fits on one.
20. **Draw the signal path left to right:** Put the inputs (power in, buttons) on the left, the core (MCU, IC) in the middle and the outputs (drivers, connectors) on the right, so the circuit reads from left to right. Keep the pins of one function on one side of a symbol (inputs left, outputs right, supply on top, GND below), and order them as the parts they connect to, so wires do not cross.

## Placement rules

21. **Place the fixed parts first:** Connectors, module headers and the power input (their positions come from the case and the pin rows) go first; then the power parts, then the small parts around them.
22. **Keep a function block together on the board:** Put the parts of one block next to each other (the whole power supply, the whole level shifter section). A block may only split when a pin position forces it.
23. **Separate analog, digital and power:** Keep analog parts (audio, sensors, lines with current peaks), the digital side (MCU, logic, data lines) and the power parts in their own areas; no digital line runs alongside a sensitive or high-current analog line, and the supply parts sit on the supply path.
24. **Decouple at the pin:** Put the decoupling capacitor of every IC within 3 mm (pad centre to pad centre) of its power pin, or as close as physically possible, joined by a short, wide trace (at least the width of rule 14 for 5V and GND). Put it on the same side as the IC (a via adds 1-2 nH) and give it its own ground via [S2]; for an STM32, ST also allows the underside of the board [S26]. This rule is a must. Modules that carry their own capacitors are exempt. Don't: let two capacitors share one ground via [S2], put that via inside the capacitor pad (rule 61), or move a capacitor away from its pin to make a tidy row (rules 10, 11 and 38 lose).
25. **Keep crystals close:** A crystal sits within 5 mm of the MCU or clock chip, and its clock traces are short, straight and of equal length.
26. **Keep the board edge free:** Keep every part body (measure the 3D body or courtyard, not only the pads: a tabbed part such as a SOT-223 lies fully inside the outline), pad and silkscreen text at least 1.27 mm (0.05 in) from the board edge, so nothing is damaged when the board is separated from a panel (5 mm if the board is cut from a panel by routed tabs; say in the README if a panel is used). For a part assembled by JLCPCB the body is at least 2.5 mm from the board edge (assembly terms, DFM note 3) [S48]; the 1.27 mm is the floor for pads, silkscreen and hand-soldered parts. Deliberate edge parts (rule 13) are the exception: check them in the JLCPCB order review. Don't: place an assembled part's body between 1.27 and 2.5 mm from the edge because the pads fit.
27. **Handle heat:** Put high-heat parts (regulators, MOSFETs, power resistors) in the airflow and give them copper to spread the heat: a dedicated pour on their tab, thermal vias, and wide traces.

## Outline rules

28. **Round the board corners:** Give every outer corner of the board a 2 mm radius (`<board borderRadius={2}>`), also for a board cut from a panel. Keep part bodies, pads and silkscreen text clear of the rounded corner; the edge clearance of rule 26 is measured from the curve.

## Schematic readability rules

Each rule names the prop that meets it. Render the schematic (`tsci export -f schematic-svg`, `rsvg-convert -w 4000`) and check it against the review list below.

29. **Group unused pins:** On a module or MCU, list the used pins first, grouped by function on the side facing their block, and put the unused pins together on the remaining side or at the end of one side (`schPinArrangement`, gaps with `schPinStyle`). Never put an unused pin between used ones. The pins stay; hiding them would change the part.
30. **Keep every part at its pin:** Draw a decoupling or bulk cap, a pull-up and a series resistor right next to the pin or rail it serves (`schX`/`schY`), close enough that the auto wire joins them (`schMaxTraceDistance`). No part floats far from its block with only supply stubs.
31. **Short, straight wires:** Put `schX`/`schY` on a 0.5 unit grid and align the pins of connected parts on one line, so the auto wire is straight; a wire has at most one bend. The router bends are not settable, so straightness comes from the placement. A connection that cannot be short becomes a label pair (rule 32).
32. **One connection style per net, with real labels:** Wire all pins of a net the same way: pin to pin, or all via `net.X`. Give every trace that draws a stub or a flag a meaningful `name` or `schDisplayLabel` (`DISCH`, `V5`), never the default `T8`, `T20`. A stub is never left without a label, and a label never sits mid-wire. Do not add `<netlabel>`: it draws a second label next to the trace one.
33. **Keep text horizontal:** Put supply pins on the top and bottom side of a symbol and signal pins on the left and right side, so stubs and their labels run horizontally. Rotate parts by 0 and 180 degrees only, except two-pin passives on a vertical path (90, 270). Never turn a connector sideways.
34. **Face connectors toward the core:** A connector at the left edge has its pins on its right side, one at the right edge has them on its left, one pin row each, pin 1 on top. Connectors of one edge share one `schX` (or `schY`).
35. **Space wires and blocks evenly:** Wires of different nets that run side by side are at least 1 unit apart; otherwise use labels. Gaps between blocks are equal (6-8 units or more), margins to the sheet frame are equal and the drawing is centred (`<schematicsheet sheetWidth sheetHeight>`). A large empty area means move the blocks closer or shrink the sheet.
36. **Put the core in the middle:** The MCU or main IC sits at the centre and each block that talks to it on the side its pins face, at a similar distance. Power block lower left, inputs left, outputs and analog (audio) right.

### Schematic review (after every render)

- No part floats away from its block (30); no unused pin between used ones (29).
- No wire with more than one bend or running past a symbol (31); no unlabelled stub, no default `T8` names, one style per net (32).
- No sideways text, no connector turned sideways, connectors face the core (33, 34).
- Equal gaps and margins, centred, no large empty area (35, 36).

## Placement and routing rules, continued

37. **Keep clearance between parts:** Keep at least 0.5 mm between the courtyards of two parts and at least 1 mm between a part and the outline of a module or header. No courtyard overlap and no pad under another part's body. `tsci check placement` reports courtyard and pad overlaps; the 0.5 mm gap itself is checked in the render. JLCPCB assembles only footprints and part gaps of IPC-7351B medium or low density, not high density [S48]. Don't: shrink a land pattern or a gap below the library footprint to save space.
38. **Put small parts on lines:** Place the passives of a cluster in a row or column on one center line with one pitch (rule 5), designators on one side (rule 8). The parts around an IC use the same offset from it, and are rotated by 0 or 90 degrees only (rule 4).
39. **Route a pair together:** Run the two lines of one signal pair (speaker +/-, differential pair) side by side on one layer with a constant gap (twice the trace width) and equal length, and let them leave the area together. Compare lengths with `tsci check trace-length <pin>`.
40. **No dangling copper:** Start and end every trace on a pad or via. No stubs, no open ends, no copper islands (remove a pour that cannot connect).
41. **Follow the power path:** Place the input connector, its bulk cap, the regulator and the loads in that order along the supply path: the bulk cap within 10 mm of the input connector, the regulator caps at the regulator (rule 24). Do not run the supply across the board and back (rules 21, 23).
42. **Mark pin 1 and polarity:** Show pin 1 of every IC and module, and the polarity of every polarized part (electrolytic or tantalum cap, LED, diode), on the silkscreen, where the part body does not cover it. Don't: trust the exported rotation of a polarized part: check placement and polarity of every SMD part in JLCPCB's production file before confirming the order. [S48]

## Silkscreen group rule

43. **Label every pad group:** Besides the pin label on each pad (rule 9), give each connector or header group one group label that names its function: `Power` for 5V+GND, `Speaker` for SPK-+SPK+, `I2C` for 5V+GND+SCL+SDA. Print it with `<silkscreentext>`, centered on the group, on the same side as the pin labels but about 1.5 mm further from the pads, in one size for all groups (larger than the pin labels), and draw a thin `<silkscreenline>` along the group, from its first to its last pad, so the extent is clear. Use short one-word names in Title case, the same as the schematic sections (rule 19). A group of one pad needs no group label.

## Part-specific layout rules

These apply only when the board carries the part. They come from vendor documents: a source tag `[S#]` refers to the Sources list of `CIRCUIT_RULES.md`, and "(convention)" marks common practice that is not in any source read. The name in brackets is the `CIRCUIT_RULES.md` rule that gives the part's value and wiring.

44. **Keep a pin's part at the pin:** A part that serves one pin, connector or load sits right at it, like the decoupling cap of rule 24: the gate series resistor close to the MOSFET (R5) [S10]; the flyback diode at the coil (Q4) [S10, S11, S33]; the USB-C CC resistors near the connector (R7) [S38]; the USB TVS as close as possible to the connector (PR-5) [S27, S38]; the ESP32 UART TX and SPI clock series parts at the chip pins (ESP-7) [S19]; the RP2040 27 ohm USB resistors close to the chip (RP-3) and the QSPI_SS resistor close to the flash (RP-4) [S27]; the WS2812B data resistor close to the first pixel (LED-5) [S39, S24]; the bulk cap of a motor, servo, relay or LED strip supply at the load connector (ESP-6) [S24].
45. **Mount MLCCs parallel to the nearest board edge** (flex cracking when the board is cut, worst for 0805 and larger). Where this turns a capacitor against rule 4, this rule wins. [S3]
46. **LDO layout (P1, P2):** Input cap directly across Vin and GND (short, low impedance); follow the manufacturer's layout and exposed-pad via instructions; take the sense line of an adjustable LDO from the intended measuring point, away from noisy copper (convention). [S15]
47. **Buck layout (P6):** The hot loop is input cap + switching FETs: put a ceramic input cap across Vin and PGND with the shortest path, on the same side as the IC; a 0.1-0.47 uF X5R/X7R bypass within about 2 cm of the input cap if needed; keep switch-node copper small (no wider than the inductor pads); put the output cap on the inductor's Vout side, away from the input cap; route feedback on the reverse side, never under or beside the inductor or switch pour; use a via array under the thermal pad; follow the datasheet layout for the ground connection (TI SLYT614: single point at the exposed pad). [S15, S16]
48. **Boost layout:** The output cap is the most important: close to the IC with short wide traces, several small caps in parallel (SLVA773: three 0805); input-cap ground at the IC power ground; the inductor close to the IC (the input cap's distance to the inductor is not critical, its current is continuous); small switch node; signal ground joined to power ground at a single point close to the PGND pin. [S17]
49. **Crystal layout (XTAL-1):** Crystal as close to the MCU as practical (rule 25: within 5 mm; on the ESP32 at least 2.7 mm from the clock pin); load caps close to the crystal and the pins, the XTALIN cap first and nearest; short traces, few vias (none on the ESP32 clock traces); no other signals under or beside the crystal; ground plane under the crystal for multilayer boards; on the 2-layer board of rule 64 that plane is the bottom pour, kept unbroken under the crystal and its traces (ESP32: ground copper on both sides of the clock trace, stitched to the pour with ground vias) [S20]. The sources disagree on the ground under the crystal: Microchip AVR042 and Espressif recommend a ground plane around or under it; one third-party RP2040 article says to exclude the copper pour, the RP2040 vendor guide gives no pour rule. Follow the guide of your MCU. [S20, S25, S27, S28]
50. **USB data pair (PR-5):** Route D+ and D- as a differential pair (rule 39): parallel, equal length, 90 ohm differential impedance (USB 2.0 spec, secondary), on a continuous ground plane, with few vias. [S27, S38] On two layers the 90 ohm is a target, not a guarantee: JLCPCB controls impedance only from 4 layers [S56]. So keep the pair short, on the top layer from connector to chip, over unbroken bottom pour. Don't: let the pair change layer, or run it across a bottom-layer hop of another net (rule 16). (convention)
51. **RF trace (RF-1):** 50 ohm +/-10 % (ESP32: the matching-capacitor stub is 100 ohm +/-10 %): microstrip over an unbroken ground plane or coplanar waveguide with ground vias on both sides; trace width from the stackup, not by guess; outer layer, no layer changes, 135 degree bends or arcs. The coplanar ground may be on the top layer (beats rule 16). [S20, S36] On two layers the RF trace is wider than on four, normally over 20 mil (0.5 mm), its width from the impedance formula for the real board thickness [S20], and the fab does not control it: JLCPCB offers controlled impedance only from 4 layers [S56]. A certified module avoids the RF trace altogether (`CIRCUIT_RULES.md` RF-1).
52. **RF via fence:** CPW ground vias at lambda/20 or less; ground vias around RF cavities at less than lambda/10 (S36 summary only, unverified); at 2.4 GHz on FR4 (eps_eff about 3.3) lambda is about 69 mm, so lambda/20 is about 3.4 mm. [S36]
53. **RF placement (RF-1):** Place parts for the shortest RF path; no parallel RF traces; keep USB, UART and clocks away from the antenna; the matching network near the chip. [S20, S36]
54. **Antenna keep-out (ESP32 module or chip):** Antenna outside the base board with its feed point near the edge, 15 mm clear in all directions in the housing, no copper or parts under or around the antenna area itself (the pour of rule 16 included), base board cut away on both sides and below the antenna; USB and UART lines far from the antenna. Don't: leave pour, traces or parts on any layer under the antenna, or seat the module in the middle of the board with its antenna over copper. [S20]
55. **ESP32 bare-chip layout:** On the 2-layer board of rule 64: parts and signal traces on the top layer, no part and as few traces as possible on the bottom, a continuous reference ground under chip, RF and crystal; power traces on the top layer (rule 15), passing between the signal pads and a reduced thermal pad. Espressif recommends 4 layers for a bare chip (layer 2 a full GND plane), so prefer a module (`CIRCUIT_RULES.md` RF-1, G9); a bare chip that cannot be laid out on two layers is a question for the user, not a reason for `layers={4}` (rule 64). Thermal pad to GND with at least 9 vias; main power traces at least 25 mil, VDD3P3 at least 20 mil, others 12-15 mil, surrounded by ground copper (on the top layer too, beats rule 16). Where these widths and rule 14 differ, use the wider. Crystal: rule 49; RF trace: rule 51. [S20]
56. **RP2040 flash (RP-4):** QSPI traces short, the flash next to the chip (the guide gives no length number). [S27]
57. **Analog ground (AVR-2, AVR-3):** For a precision ADC keep analog and digital ground apart and join them at one point only when the part has a separate AGND (the ATmega328P has none: use the analog ground plane of datasheet figure 24-9). This extends rule 23. [S25]
58. **Isolation barrier (ISO-3):** Meet the creepage and clearance of `CIRCUIT_RULES.md` ISO-3 on the board: keep the isolator pads from shrinking the creepage; slots or grooves in the board increase creepage; separate high-voltage and low-voltage circuits physically and electrically; round corners on high-voltage traces; conformal coating may allow shorter distances (IEC 60664-3, not read). [S32, S33]
59. **Relay layout (RLY-1):** Flyback diode at the coil (rule 44); keep high-voltage contact traces and low-voltage coil/logic apart with the creepage of `CIRCUIT_RULES.md` ISO-3. [S33]
60. **IR receiver mounting (IR-3):** Mount the receiver where the window and viewing angle fit the case, away from switching noise (convention).

## Manufacturing rules

These apply to every board that is reflow-assembled. They come from the assembly terms of JLCPCB [S48] and the assembly guideline of a PCB maker [S49].

61. **No via in or at an SMD pad:** Keep every via and plated hole at least 0.25 mm (pad edge to hole edge) away from an SMD pad, with solder mask between them [S49]. An open via in a pad drains the paste down the hole and pulls heat from that pad: skewed parts, open joints and tombstoning, and JLCPCB takes no responsibility for the result [S48]. The thermal vias under an exposed pad are the exception: place them as the part's datasheet shows (rules 46, 47, 55). Don't: drop the ground via of a decoupling capacitor into its pad to meet rule 24; put it beside the pad. Don't order filled and capped via-in-pad to get around this rule: it is a multilayer process (JLCPCB's default only from 6 layers) [S56].
62. **Feed both pads of a small part alike:** Both pads of a two-pin passive get the same copper: one trace of the same width on each pad, running at least 0.25 mm before it widens or enters a pour. A pad that sits in a large copper area, or has more or wider traces than the other, heats differently; the solder on the two pads then does not melt at the same moment and the part is pulled upright (tombstoning) [S49, S48]. Where rule 14 gives one pad a wide power trace, neck it to the width of the other pad's trace for the last 0.25 mm or more; a decoupling capacitor with two equally wide traces (rule 24) is balanced. Don't: embed one pad solid in a pour while the other hangs on a thin trace.
63. **No silkscreen under small parts:** Keep silkscreen out from under parts of 0603 size and smaller, above all over a trace that runs between their pads: copper, solder mask and legend stacked there can stand higher than the pads and lift the part [S49]. Silkscreen never lies on a pad (rule 8).

## Layer and via rules

These are the scope of the whole file: every board is a 2-layer board, and vias are what make two layers enough. The numbers come from the capabilities page of JLCPCB [S56] and the two-layer notes of Espressif [S20].

64. **Two copper layers, each with a job:** Build every board on two copper layers and say so in the code: `<board layers={2}>` (the tscircuit default, written out). The top layer carries all parts and the signal and power traces. The bottom layer carries the GND pour (rule 16) and short crossing segments (rule 65), and no parts: the same split Espressif gives for a two-layer board (components and traces on top, the bottom kept for ground with as few traces as possible) [S20]. There is no inner layer, no blind or buried via and no impedance-controlled stackup (JLCPCB offers that from 4 layers) [S56]. When a board does not route: move or turn parts, cross with vias (rule 65), enlarge the board, or use a module in place of a bare chip (`CIRCUIT_RULES.md` G9). If it still does not fit, stop and ask the user. Only the README Requirements can name another layer count or allow parts on the bottom. Don't: set `layers={1}` (no plated holes, so no vias and no pour under the parts [S56]) or `layers={4}` and above to get a route through, or move a part to the bottom side to make room. (convention)
65. **Use vias: they are what makes two layers work:** Reach for a via wherever it shortens a path. What a via buys on a 2-layer board:
    - **Crossing:** a trace that has to cross another one goes down through a via, runs a short segment on the bottom under the other trace and comes back up through a second via. Two vias and a few mm of bottom copper replace a detour around the board or a 0 ohm jumper (`CIRCUIT_RULES.md` G7).
    - **Ground:** a GND via beside a top-layer GND pad reaches the pour (rule 16) within a millimetre or two, so the return path is short; put one close to the ground pad of every capacitor [S20], each decoupling capacitor with its own (rule 24) [S2].
    - **Stitching:** GND vias tie top-layer ground copper (the guard copper beside a crystal, an RF trace or a UART line: rules 49, 51, 55) to the pour, so that it is ground and not an island (rule 40) [S20].
    - **Heat:** vias under a tab or an exposed pad carry heat to the copper on the other side (rules 27, 46, 47, 55).
    - **Cost:** none. Plated through vias are drilled and plated with every 2-layer board, and JLCPCB charges extra only for holes smaller than the 0.3 mm of rule 66 [S56]. Count the length of the detour, not the number of vias.

    The limits are few and each has its own rule: a supply net stays on one layer (rule 15), no via in or at an SMD pad (rule 61), none on a crystal's clock traces (rule 49) or an RF trace (rule 51), and no layer change on a USB pair (rule 50). Don't: detour a signal across the top layer, or add a 0 ohm jumper, to save a via; run a long bottom-layer trace where two vias and a short hop do the job (it cuts the pour, rule 16); leave a GND pad on a long ground trace when a via beside it reaches the pour. (convention)
66. **Size and place vias for the fab:** Use one via size on the whole board: 0.6 mm pad with a 0.3 mm hole, the default of the tscircuit autorouter, which JLCPCB makes at no extra charge. JLCPCB's limits for a 2-layer board: pad at least 0.1 mm (preferably 0.15 mm) larger than the hole, preferred smallest hole 0.2 mm, via hole to via hole at least 0.2 mm, via hole to trace at least 0.2 mm [S56]. Keep a via 0.25 mm from every SMD pad (rule 61). A power line that has to change layer (rule 15) takes at least two vias side by side [S20]. Don't: shrink a via to squeeze it in: a 0.2 or 0.25 mm hole with a pad under 0.45 mm, and any 0.1 or 0.15 mm hole, costs more [S56]. Don't specify blind, buried or filled and capped vias: those are multilayer and HDI processes [S56].

### PCB review (after every layout change)

- All bodies, tabs included, inside the outline (26); no courtyard closer than 0.5 mm (37).
- Small parts on lines, designators on one side, text horizontal (1, 7, 8, 38).
- Pairs parallel and equal (39); no stubs (40); supply path short (41).
- Pin 1 and polarity marked (42); every pad group has a group label (43).
- Part-specific rules checked for every part family on the board: pin-serving parts, MLCCs at the edge, regulators, crystal, USB, RF and antenna, isolation, relay (44-60).
- No via in or within 0.25 mm of an SMD pad; both pads of small passives fed alike; no silkscreen under small parts (61-63).
- Two copper layers only (`layers={2}`): parts on top, the bottom is GND pour plus short hops, and the pour is still one piece (16, 64).
- Vias used wherever they shorten a path: at crossings, beside every GND pad, under hot pads; one size (0.6 mm pad, 0.3 mm hole); two vias where a power line changes layer (15, 65, 66).
- No `Don't:` of an applicable rule is on the board: no trace across a gap in the pour, no acute junction, no assembled body closer than 2.5 mm to the edge, no detour or 0 ohm jumper in place of a via, no long bottom-layer trace (16, 17, 26, 65).
